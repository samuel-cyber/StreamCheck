Citizen photos in. Public-health intelligence out.

Riparia is the validation layer between citizen science apps and the health systems that need their data. Every observation is assessed against published urban-stream indicators by AI — and confirmed by a human reviewer — before it becomes standards-grade HL7 FHIR data.

01

Citizens observe

A photo and a few words, submitted from the field through the citizen form or a partner app.

02

AI assesses

A vision model checks the submission against OneAquaHealth's published indicators and returns a confidence score with a plain-language rationale.

03

Humans decide

Reviewers see the evidence and the AI's reasoning, then approve or reject. Nothing moves without a person.

04

Systems consume

Approved observations are committed as HL7 FHIR — interoperable with municipal and public-health platforms.

Grounded in published research, not guesswork

The OneAquaHealth project found pharmaceutical contamination at the majority of monitored urban stream sites, and degraded streams hosting disease-vector insects. Riparia's assessment criteria are those findings' early-warning indicators — not generic pollution language.

  • Riparian vegetation degradationLoss of streamside plant cover — habitat and erosion signal
  • Artificial light at nightNear-water lighting that disrupts aquatic ecosystems
  • Trash & debrisVisible pollution and physical contamination
  • Chemical / pharmaceutical signsFoam, sheen, unusual color, odor cues
